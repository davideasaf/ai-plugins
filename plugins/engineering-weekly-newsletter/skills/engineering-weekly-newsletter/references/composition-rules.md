# Composition rules

## Audience and tone

- **Executive:** lead with consequence, risk, decision relevance, and one useful number. Remove implementation detail that does not change the decision.
- **Engineering leader:** explain what changed, why teams should care, what to test or ask, and what remains unproven.
- **Developer:** lead with versions, APIs, architecture, demonstrated behavior, limitations, and migration or evaluation implications.
- **General technical:** define unavoidable jargon and keep one idea per sentence.

Use direct, natural prose. Name who or what acted. Prefer concrete verbs and short paragraphs. Avoid management shorthand, promotional superlatives, and a release-ledger feel.

## Newsletter shape

1. Title, exact window, and audience.
2. One-sentence weekly thesis.
3. Three to five lead stories, ordered by consequence.
4. Optional quick hits for bounded secondary developments.
5. Practical questions or actions for the audience.
6. Optional watchlist for credible items that are not yet ready to report as outcomes.
7. Review checklist.

For every lead story:

- `What changed` states the event, date, actor, and supported publication state.
- `Why it matters` translates the consequence without inflating adoption or impact.
- `Sources` links the strongest first-party evidence and, when useful, independent or community context.

Keep internal ledger labels out of reader copy. Write “maintainers proposed” or “practitioners reported” instead of `[proposal]` or `[community signal]`.

## Community voice

Use community reaction when it adds implementation experience, disagreement, or a sharp practical concern. Attribute it to the named person, channel, or community and link the source. Do not write “developers agree” from a small sample. Do not let a viral reaction outweigh contradictory first-party evidence.

## Subtraction pass

Remove:

- stories with no in-window catalyst;
- repeated background that did not change;
- unsupported availability, adoption, or impact claims;
- a second implication that merely restates the first;
- clever closing lines that carry no information;
- empty template sections;
- research diagnostics and acquisition narration;
- links that do not support the adjacent claim.

Aim for a three-minute read unless the user requests another length. Depth of research does not require depth of prose.

## Review and handoff

Put unresolved dates, source conflicts, unclear availability, weak attribution, missing benchmark methods, and uncertain audience relevance in the review checklist. Do not silently delete them.

Return:

- exact inclusive window and whether the final day is partial;
- sources and editorial desks checked;
- degraded or unavailable lanes;
- stories deliberately excluded and why;
- unresolved claims;
- validator result;
- a clear reminder that publication or sending requires explicit authorization.
